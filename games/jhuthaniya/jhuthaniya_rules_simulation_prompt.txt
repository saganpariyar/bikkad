SECTION 1: COMPLETE OFFICIAL RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. OVERVIEW & OBJECTIVE
   - Name: Jhuthaniya (also known globally as Bluff, Cheat, or I Doubt It).
   - Players: 2 to 7 players (ideal with 3 to 5 players).
   - Deck: Standard 52-card deck (no jokers). For 6-7 players, a double deck can be used.
   - Objective: Be among the players who get rid of all their cards.
   - Loser: The game continues until only 1 player is left holding cards. That final
     player is designated the sole "Loser" (Jhuthaniya).

2. SETUP & DEAL
   - A dealer is randomly chosen.
   - The entire deck is shuffled and dealt clockwise, one card at a time, until all
     cards are distributed.
   - Depending on the number of players, some may receive one card more than others
     (e.g., in a 4-player game, all receive 13 cards; in a 3-player game, two get 17
     and one gets 18). This asymmetry is a normal part of the game.
   - Players inspect and organize their hands privately.

3. ROUND STRUCTURE & TURN ACTIONS
   - The starting player (or the winner of the previous game) begins.
   - On their turn, the active player places 1 to 4 cards face down into the center pot
     (the discard pile) and declares their claim out loud:
       * Format: "[Count] [Rank]" (e.g., "3 Begams / Queens", "2 Kings", "1 Ace").
   - The player may tell the truth (playing actual cards of that rank) or bluff
     (placing unrelated cards face down).

4. THE CHALLENGE WINDOW ("JHUTH!" / "BLUFF!")
   - Before the next player takes their turn, any other player (or the immediate next
     player, depending on house preference) can challenge the claim by shouting:
     "Jhuth!" (Bluff!) or "Check!".
   - If multiple players challenge simultaneously, the challenger seated nearest to the
     left of the playing player takes priority.

5. RESOLVING A CHALLENGE
   - Inspection:
     * Standard Rule: The challenger reveals the played cards (or designates one card from
       the played batch to inspect).
   - Case A: The claim was a BLUFF (the cards do not match the declared rank):
     * The bluffing player is caught.
     * The bluffing player MUST pick up the ENTIRE center pot and add it to their hand.
     * The next turn begins with the challenger or the player to the left of the bluffer.
   - Case B: The claim was TRUTHFUL (the cards match the declared rank):
     * The challenger was mistaken.
     * The challenger MUST pick up the ENTIRE center pot into their hand.
     * The honest player retains the advantage, and the next turn proceeds.

6. PASS / SKIP
   - If no player chooses to challenge the claim, players say "Pass" or "Skip".
   - The cards remain face down in the pot.
   - The turn passes clockwise to the next player, who now plays their own set of cards.

7. WINNING & ELIMINATION
   - When a player places their last card(s) face down, they announce they are out.
   - However, they are not officially safe until the challenge window passes.
     * If someone challenges and the player bluffed on their last card, they collect the
       entire pot and remain in the game.
     * If the play is unchallenged, or if challenged and proven honest, the player is
       officially SAFE (exits the game as a winner).
   - Gameplay continues among the remaining players until only ONE player remains holding
     cards. That player is the LOSER.

```text
Build a robust, modular, turn-based multiplayer card game engine in Python 3.10+ for the traditional Indian card game "Jhuthaniya" (also known as Bluff / Cheat / I Doubt It).

1. Core Game Mechanics:
   - Support 2 to 7 players (human player vs. configurable AI bots).
   - Standard 52-card deck (suits: Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades; ranks: 2-10, J, Q, K, A).
   - Deal cards evenly among all active players.
   - Maintain private hands for all players and a central hidden discard pile (`center_pot`).

2. Turn Protocol & Actions:
   - Active player selects 1 to 4 cards from their hand to place face down into `center_pot`.
   - Active player declares a claim: `(claimed_count: int, claimed_rank: str)` (e.g., 3 "Q").
   - Challenge Phase:
     * Clockwise rotation allows other players to either call "JHUTH" (Challenge) or "PASS".
     * If challenged: Reveal the played cards.
       - If ANY card does not match the claimed rank -> BLUFF DETECTED: Claimant picks up all cards in `center_pot`.
       - If ALL cards match the claimed rank -> TRUTH PROVEN: Challenger picks up all cards in `center_pot`.
     * If all players pass: Cards remain face down in `center_pot`. Turn moves to the next player.

3. Win & Elimination Logic:
   - When a player places their last card, they must survive the Challenge Phase.
   - If challenged and bluffing: They take the pot and remain in the game.
   - If unchallenged or truthful: They are marked 'SAFE' (exited) and removed from turn order.
   - The game continues until exactly ONE player remains with cards. That player is declared the 'LOSER' (Jhuthaniya).

4. AI Bot Heuristics:
   - Honest Preference: If the bot holds cards of the target/desired rank, it plays them truthfully.
   - Bluff Generator: If the bot lacks the desired rank, it picks its lowest/useless cards to bluff.
   - Challenge Evaluator (Card Counting & Probability):
     * Calculate impossibility: If Bot holds X cards of Rank R, and Opponent claims Y cards of Rank R, where X + Y > 4, Challenge Probability = 100%.
     * Risk tolerance: If the `center_pot` has > 10 cards, challenge only on high certainty. If the pot has < 3 cards, challenge more aggressively.
     * Endgame suspicion: Increase challenge rate by 70% if an opponent claims to play their final cards.

5. Deliverables:
   - Clean, object-oriented design (`Card`, `Deck`, `Player`, `AIBot`, `JhuthaniyaGame`).
   - Rich CLI interface with ANSI color codes or clear ASCII tables showing player card counts, pot size, turn history, and challenge verdicts.
   - Unit tests covering: honest verification, caught bluffs, win-state transitions, and last-card exit edge cases.
```
